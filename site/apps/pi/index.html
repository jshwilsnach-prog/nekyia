Two counts on a circle

A line asked to become a world. No last digit. One being counts from a guessed end — a bound, not a finish. One being counts from the perfect now — the unit underfoot, not the whole circle. If either stops, the wheel seizes.

from a guessed end

Circumscribed. Always too much. It knows a bound it has not earned.

from the unit underfoot

Inscribed. Always too little. It knows only what it has stepped.

The meeting, as far as the two have figured it. Lit digits are agreed by both. Nobody holds them alone.

Archimedes, in exact integers: the semiperimeters of the circumscribed and inscribed n-gons are the harmonic and geometric means of the previous pair. Sides double each step — 6, 12, 24, 48 — and the two counts squeeze a value neither of them owns. Arithmetic runs at eighty decimal places in whole numbers, so no float rounds the meeting for you.
